*A letter home from the Camino — part two. ([Part one is here](/2026/11/24/the-light-in-their-faces/).)*

Somewhere on the road, with nothing to do but walk, a question settled on me and would not leave. What does an Olympic athlete have in common with a Navy SEAL and a peregrino?

A peregrino is a pilgrim, one of the walkers on the Camino. On the face of it the three could hardly be more different. One trains for a medal. One trains for war. One walks, often with no clearer aim than to walk — to simplify life. And yet I think they are doing, at bottom, the same thing. Each is forging something. Each builds will, resilience, perseverance, the capacity to keep going when everything in you is asking to stop. They are, whether they would put it this way or not, imitating the great spiritual design.

Because the forging is the point. I have come to see it is why we are here at all.

There is an old text that says this better than I can. It is called the Emerald Tablet, attributed to the legendary Hermes Trismegistus, and it is one of the seed documents of the whole Western esoteric tradition. Here it is in full, in Isaac Newton's own translation, made around 1680:

> *Tis true without lying, certain and most true.*  
> *That which is below is like that which is above, and that which is above is like that which is below, to do the miracle of one only thing.*  
> *And as all things have been and arose from one by the mediation of one, so all things have their birth from this one thing by adaptation.*  
> *The Sun is its father, the moon its mother, the wind hath carried it in its belly, the earth is its nurse.*  
> *The father of all perfection in the whole world is here.*  
> *Its force or power is entire if it be converted into earth.*  
> *Separate thou the earth from the fire, the subtle from the gross, sweetly with great industry.*  
> *It ascends from the earth to the heaven, and again it descends to the earth, and receives the force of things superior and inferior.*  
> *By this means you shall have the glory of the whole world, and thereby all obscurity shall fly from you.*  
> *Its force is above all force, for it vanquishes every subtle thing and penetrates every solid thing.*  
> *So was the world created.*  
> *From this are and do come admirable adaptations, whereof the means is here in this.*  
> *Hence I am called Hermes Trismegistus, having the three parts of the philosophy of the whole world.*  
> *That which I have said of the operation of the Sun is accomplished and ended.*
>
> — Hermes Trismegistus (attributed), the Emerald Tablet, trans. Isaac Newton, Keynes MS 28 (c. 1680)

Read past the strangeness of the language and a shape emerges. Something descends into matter, into earth. It is worked on. The subtle is separated from the gross, "sweetly with great industry." It ascends, and then it descends again, and only in that return does it receive "the force of things superior and inferior" and come into "the glory of the whole world." That is the forging. It happens here, in the earth, in the body, under load. It is not done anywhere else.

This is why we come to earth. Not to be comfortable. To be forged. To meet obstacles, to come up against our limits, to be worn and tested and, slowly, made stronger. And I do not mean stronger in the body, though the Camino does that too, just like the athlete and the seal. I mean stronger in the thing that survives. In forging ourselves we take our small place in a great hierarchy of spiritual beings, as servants of something higher than ourselves. The athlete, the soldier, the pilgrim, each in their own arena, is in service to something beyond them. That is the family resemblance.

I often hear people say to me, with great certainty, "I'm not coming back after this life." I understand why they say it. But I have to tell you, gently, that it is not really them speaking. It is the little ego, worn down by the defeats and the hurts and the losses that everyday life hands out, and out of that exhaustion it makes a profoundly incorrect statement.

It helps to remember what the word suffer actually means. It comes from the Latin *sufferre*, "to bear up under, to undergo" (from *sub*, under, and *ferre*, to carry). To suffer is simply to undergo something. Whether a given life looks hard or easy from the outside is almost beside the point. We all undergo. We all suffer, in that older and larger sense. Which is exactly why it is so important not to judge where another person is on their road. On the Camino you meet people from every walk of life, and over the weeks the ones you keep meeting become your Camino family, your peregrino family. Lifelong friendships come out of it, because the experience you have shared is so profound, so intimate, so personal.

Here is the key, and it is not a comfortable one. Most of us are not forged enough. You see it the moment real hardship arrives and people fall apart, because there was not yet enough in them to hold the weight. I will put it plainly. Most of us are only half-baked. It is only by facing hardship, the fire of life, that we forge something solid enough to survive the disassembly that comes after death.

This is why, if you ever find yourself working with a genuine spiritual master, you will notice a strange rhythm to it. The master sets you to work on projects, the longer the better, then throws you into intense spiritual practice, then puts you back onto projects. It looks inefficient. It is nothing of the kind. Something is being forged in you that could be forged no other way.

Walking the Camino forges the will. By the end it was as though I had been handed a Ferrari engine, idling high, all that gathered will revving in me with nowhere in particular to go. And I will be honest about what came afterward, because it is part of the truth of it. When I finished, all that unleashed will left me feeling oddly flat. Unengaged. Food I loved had lost its grip, preferences I had held, things I had been certain were important, all of it had quietly lost its significance. The fire had burned off a great deal of what I had taken to be me.

And that, in the end, is what the Emerald Tablet describes so beautifully. The whole of a life is this: a forging here on earth, a returning to the high spiritual spaces, and then a coming back, reincarnating again and again, until at last we are strong enough to withstand the unravelling that waits on the far side of death, and to stand within it as the small, bright, undying flame we have always been.

So when the ego says I'm not coming back, smile at it kindly. It has simply mistaken its own pain and tiredness for the truth. We come back. We come back until the work is done.

Beyond belief, into knowing. Even this, it turns out, you can begin to find out on foot.
