*The buried light in the base matter, and what direct knowing makes of it.*

The last essay ended at a threshold. When belief finally ripens into knowing, I said, what it comes to know is not an abstraction — it is a light, particular and buried, already alight in you. This one is about that light: what it is, where it hides, and what the old alchemists swore it could become if you stopped stepping over it.

They began every Work with what they called the *prima materia*, the first matter — and they described it, almost gleefully, as worthless. Something trodden underfoot, thrown out with the household rubbish, so common that nobody troubles to bend down for it. The joke, which was not a joke, was that the whole opus depended on this discarded stuff, because sealed inside the base matter was a seed of light. The alchemists were not being modest. They were telling you where to look: not up, in some rarefied elsewhere, but down, in the part of yourself you'd written off.

They had a word for that seed. Khunrath, writing in 1595, spoke of the *scintillae* — the "fiery sparks of the world-soul, of the light of nature, dispersed throughout the structure of the great world." Paracelsus and others called it the *lumen naturae*, the light of nature; some spoke of an invisible sun hidden inside the human being. Jung, reading them closely, called these the "germinal luminosities" — sparks shining from the darkness of the psyche, many little lights that were somehow one light. The Gnostics, older and blunter, said a fragment of the divine had fallen into matter and lay there in exile, and that salvation was not being forgiven but *waking up* — the spark remembering what it was. And Meister Eckhart, who nearly went to the stake for it, called it the *Seelenfünklein*, the little spark of the soul, and said that something in the soul is uncreated and God-identical.

Different centuries, different languages, one stubborn claim: there is a light in you that is not yours in the way your opinions are yours. It is older than your personality. And it does nothing much until it is worked.

Here the alchemists get practical, and they do it in colour. The Work passes through three stains, and anyone who has actually turned toward their interior life will recognise all three.

The first is the *nigredo* — the blackening. Everything rots. What looked solid goes to pieces; the self you'd assembled comes apart. This is the phase I've elsewhere called [purification](/2026/05/03/the-three-phases-of-spiritual-awakening/), and it is the one nobody wants: uncomfortable, unstable, occasionally humiliating. But notice the sequence the alchemists insist on — the spark is only *seen* against the black. You cannot make out a single point of light at noon. It takes the darkening for the luminosity to show at all. The dissolution is not the enemy of the spark; it is what uncovers it.

The second is the *albedo* — the whitening. After the rot, a washing. The spark, freed of the muck it was buried in, shows clean — silver, lunar, a first light like the grey before dawn. This is the quieter thing that comes after the worst of the purification: not fireworks, just a clarity that wasn't there before. You begin to see *by* the light instead of merely believing it is there.

The third is the *rubedo* — the reddening. And this is the one that matters most for what we're tracing, because whitening is not the end. A clean light can still be lost; a dawn can cloud over. The red stage is where the light is *fixed* — congealed, made to stay. The volatile is made permanent. The spark stops being a visitor and becomes a structure. The alchemists called that finished structure the *lapis*, the Stone — and, tellingly, "the stone which the builders rejected," and also "the stone which is not a stone," because it is nothing you could put in your pocket. It is the hidden stone *in you*: the light of the first matter, gathered, awakened, and set.

Now put the two accounts side by side. The three phases of an awakening — the glimpse, the purification, the integration — and the three colours of the opus — black, white, red — are the same road walked in different clothes. And both traditions tell you the same inconvenient truth: it is not a single pass. You blacken and whiten and redden, and then some deeper layer presents itself and you begin again. The Stone is built in coats, not in a stroke.

What congeals the light? Here is the whole point, and it is why my school is named as it is. The agent of the fixing is *gnosis* — direct knowing. Belief cannot do it; belief keeps the light at arm's length, a rumour of warmth. Only the witnessing or experiencing of the thing, first-hand, gathers the scattered sparks and makes them cohere — closer to building muscle than to receiving a gift: it grows only by use, by repetition, by return. Combine that with a huge dose of grace. Gnosis is not a neighbour of the Stone. It is what the Stone is *made of*. And it works by witnessing rather than reasoning: what is seen first-hand is thereby fixed — through the witnessing, the fire congeals. This is the move the whole path has been circling: knowing does not merely *find* the divine spark — poured back in, coat after coat, knowing is what the spark hardens into.

And what you are left with, when the reddening holds, is not a mood or a good season. The tradition I teach from is unembarrassed about this: what you are forging is an imperishable individuality — the enduring centre the soul carries when everything borrowed has been set down. Steiner would call it the eternal in the human being; the Clairvision stream calls it the body of immortality; the alchemists called it the Stone. I won't tidy those into a single equation — the East and the West mean genuinely different things by the Self that survives, and that tension is honest work for another day. But they agree on the direction: a light buried in the ordinary, worked through the dark and the dawn, set at last into something that lasts.

That is where this goes next — because a Stone that lasts is no use if it flees the world. The reddening is warm, solar, embodied; the Stone is meant to come *back down* into a life — the little self, the everyday ego, made subservient to the Self, the fixed fire it was always meant to serve. But that is the following essay.

For now it is enough to know the spark is real, and that it has been waiting — under the rubbish you stepped over — for you to stop believing in it and start knowing it.

Beyond belief — into knowing. It was always the only journey.
