Two of the strangest instructions in the Western tradition were never meant to be read as words at all. They were hidden inside words — acronyms, carved where you'd least expect to find a map of the soul.

The first, **VITRIOL**, sat on the alchemist's jar of corrosive acid. Spell it out and it opens: *Visita Interiora Terrae Rectificando Invenies Occultum Lapidem* — "Visit the interior of the earth; by rectifying what you find, you will discover the hidden stone." Basil Valentine set it down some five centuries ago, and it has been quietly unsettling people ever since.

The second, **INRI**, was nailed above a dying man. Everyone knows the church reading — *Iesus Nazarenus Rex Iudaeorum*, Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews. The Rosicrucian alchemists looked at the same four letters and read something else in them: *Igne Natura Renovatur Integra* — "By fire, nature is renewed whole."

One points down, into the earth. The other up, into flame. Between them sits the whole of a practice.

**Down first.** Notice what VITRIOL does *not* say. It doesn't say ascend, transcend, rise above the messy human stuff into some serene blue sky. It says *visit the interior of the earth* — your earth. The body. The buried. The rooms in yourself you paved over long ago because it was easier to build on top of them than to go in. Everyone polishes the four rooms of their house. Nobody ever enters the hidden fifth room.

And the word is not chosen gently. Vitriol is acid. It dissolves. This is the movement Jung, who spent the back half of his life reading the alchemists as a mirror held up to the psyche, called the *nigredo* — the blackening, the *solutio*, the stage where what felt solid comes apart in your hands. Sit long enough and honestly enough, and things surface: the old grief, the resentment you'd filed under "dealt with," the fear you've been outrunning since you were young. Oh, if only I'd been paid a dollar for every time I heard the sentence, "but I dealt with that," or, "but I forgave that person." Every instinct says *go up, go to the light*. VITRIOL says the opposite. Go down. Let it dissolve.

But look closely at the middle of the phrase — *rectificando*, by rectifying. To rectify is to purify, to set straight, to witness. The acid isn't destruction for its own sake; it strips what's false so that what's true can be set right. And the promise at the end is the quiet shock of the whole thing: *invenies occultum lapidem* — you will find the **hidden** stone. Not manufacture. Not import from some holier place. Uncover. The gold was always down there, under everything you dissolved to reach it.

I'll be honest — I spent a good stretch of my own practice in love with the descent, mistaking the drama of dissolution for depth. There's a particular vanity in the person who is forever "doing their shadow work." But a puddle is not enlightened. If all you ever do is dissolve, you become beautifully undefended and completely unable to hold a life together. Dissolution is not the destination. It's the ground you clear.

Which is where the second fire comes in.

*By fire, nature is renewed whole.* Here's the thing worth sitting with: it's the **same fire**. Vitriol's corrosive burn and INRI's renewing flame are not two elements — they're one fire seen from two ends of the work. The same heat that dissolves the false tempers the true. Fire doesn't only destroy; it forges, it anneals, it makes a blade hold its edge.

Read the cross this way and it stops being only a historical execution and becomes a diagram. The figure on it is the personal ego — the small self, lowercase, the one that spent your whole life certain it was the centre of things. What the fire asks of it is not annihilation but transformation. Something does die there: your little ego, your sense of me that mistook itself for the whole. And what is *renewed — integra, entire —* is the Self, in Jung's language; the Ego in the language of the Western Esoteric Tradition, the true centre, the I AM. Not a shinier version of the personality. A different centre of gravity altogether.

And something has to remain to *be* that centre. This is where a good deal of contemporary spirituality loses its footing, chasing the dissolution of the self as though the goal were to disappear. But empty yourself out entirely — dissolve the last one who can say *I am* — and there is no one left home for the renewal to reach. The I AM is not the obstacle to the work. It is what the work is for, and what receives what the fire brings.

In practice, this building movement is quieter than the dissolving, and far less talked about — dissolution photographs better. But it's the half that actually changes a life. Steadiness returns after the *nigredo*, though it's a new kind of steadiness — not the old brittle certainty that cracked under the acid, something tempered, something that has been through fire and knows it. You re-enter the ordinary day. You do the washing up, cut the lawn. And the renewed nature carries the fire into those ordinary hours instead of needing to escape them.

Now — why were these hidden inside acronyms at all, rather than written plainly on the first page? Because you cannot believe your way through vitriol. You can read every book ever written on the *nigredo* and remain entirely solid, entirely undissolved, the stone still buried and invisible to you. The formula reveals itself only to the one who actually goes in.

So let me say plainly where the going-in happens.

## The vessel

The alchemists were emphatic about one piece of equipment, and it wasn't the fire. It was the vessel — the sealed glass the work takes place inside. No vessel, no operation. Heat something in the open air and you don't get transformation, you get a mess and a smell.

Meditation is the vessel. That is not a metaphor I'm reaching for; it is the practical difference between an alchemy and an accident. Dissolution will come to you whether or not you have a practice — life administers vitriol to everyone eventually, in the form of loss, illness, betrayal, the slow arrival of middle age. Uncontained, that dissolution is simply suffering, and people are broken by it every day. Held inside a vessel, the same dissolution becomes the first movement of the work. The material is identical. The container is what changes its meaning.

Building the walls of that vessel is the whole of what the tradition calls the Great Work, and it has many facets: a particular style of meditation held in conjunction with aspiration, the building of will, the awakening of spiritual vision, the seeking of truth — and, among these, ritual.

## The acid, in practice: not this, not that

Ask what dissolution actually consists of, sitting down, and the answer is older and simpler than the alchemists. [Not this, not this](/2026/07/14/not-this-not-this/) — *neti neti*.

A thought arrives. Not this. A sensation in the left knee. Not this. A wave of irritation about something said to you on Thursday. Not that. Not because any of it is bad — this isn't a purge, and the contents aren't your enemy — but because none of it is you. Each time you decline to be the content, a little more of what you had been standing on dissolves. That is vitriol applied. The acid isn't something you import into the sitting; it's the steady refusal to keep identifying with what passes.

And it doesn't stay on the cushion, which is the part people are unprepared for. The same blade turns on your life. The role you play at work: not this. The reputation you maintain: not that. The story you've told about your childhood so many times it has worn a groove: not this either. The grievance you have been carrying so long it has become a piece of furniture you'd miss if it went. Not that. It goes on rather further than is comfortable, and that is precisely why it works. You feel the emptying as if you had more space inside you. You feel like you reside deeper inside yourself somehow.

## Building spiritual muscle

Here is where most accounts stop, and it's the reason so many people believe that meditation is not for them. They don't go far enough. Deconstruction is only half the operation. Something is being **built**.

In this lineage we speak of subtle bodies. You already have one — everyone does; it is your life force and the vehicle of your thoughts and feelings, and they were issued with you at birth. What practice builds is not a new body but a *transformed* one: that same vehicle restructured, made able to hold what it could not hold before. Plainer word: spiritual muscle — and muscle is never handed to you ready-made, it is tissue you already have, restructured under load, by repetition and recovery and load again. Not by reading about it. Not by agreeing with it. What you are actually strengthening is the capacity to hold your own energy so that something can land, be forged — the real part of you to guide the decisions you make, the flow of your life, becoming the master of your own consciousness instead of being swept along by it. Every sitting in which you stay present with something you would rather stand up and walk away from is one repetition. There is no way to accumulate those in advance, and no way to borrow anyone else's.

This is what *fixatio* means in the alchemical texts — fixing, coagulation. It is not addition; nothing new is brought in from outside. It is binding. Dissolution frees something volatile and mobile and easily lost, and fixing is the slow work of making it stay. Everyone who has practised for any length of time knows the failure mode: the extraordinary hour on the cushion that has entirely evaporated by the time you have driven home and someone has taken your parking space. That is unfixed mercury — real, and gone. The building movement is what makes an insight survive contact with an ordinary Tuesday.

And notice what that asks us to correct about the word *deconstruction*. People hear it and picture annihilation — the self broken up and got rid of for good. But you cannot pour anything into a cup you have smashed. The structure that holds your thoughts and emotions is not the enemy; it is the vessel — the chalice — and the work is to empty it, not to break it. You empty the cup so that something can be received into it. Dismantle the false, and keep the chalice.

Which is also why *solve et coagula* is written as one formula and not two steps you complete once. The spiral goes round again. Dissolve deeper, fix firmer, and again.

## How you experience this

Let me describe what this is actually like, since so little of the literature will.

Engaging your meditation process reveals a fire above your head over time. This fire seeks to descend, moving into your body and psyche — opening, cleansing, purifying. It will seek to pass right through you, entering the realms far below you. That is not a visualisation you perform and it is not a metaphor I am offering you for something else. It is a perception, a lived experience, and it arrives in its own time.

Over time you might also experience a growing sense of space inside yourself, a sense of harmony, of alignment within yourself. Issues you experience in your life drop away, or resolve in an easy manner — a sense of knowing, or insight, for you to rest upon and let drive your life. Instead of reacting to tensions inside yourself, you learn to hold them, and to find that there is always a superior way to move forward and resolve those tensions.

Read that last sentence again, because it is the whole of the building movement in ordinary language. Not the absence of tension — the capacity to hold it. The tensions still arrive. What changes is that they no longer have to be discharged the moment they appear, and in the holding, something wiser than your reaction has room to show up.

I want to be clear with you about what this is like, because I think the field does people a disservice by promising serenity. It is brutal. There are sittings where grief arrives without any warning or invitation and you simply have to stay with it. There are long dry months where nothing whatever happens and every part of you argues for giving it up. Dismantling your own scaffolding while you are still standing on it is not a comfortable procedure and I won't pretend otherwise.

It is also, and at the very same time, the most enriching thing I have ever done. Not alternately — not brutal on Tuesday and enriching on Friday. Both at once, in the same hour, which is a thing almost impossible to convey to someone who hasn't been there and instantly recognisable to someone who has. The acid and the fire were never two.

That is the whole of it, and it's why I keep returning to the same four words: **Beyond Belief — Into Knowing.** Belief stands at the mouth of the cave with a torch and an excellent vocabulary. Knowing goes down into the earth, lets the acid do its slow work, and comes back up through the fire changed.

## What it all comes down to

The tradition hands us an enormous apparatus — the acronyms, the colours from black through peacock to red, the crow and the swan and the phoenix, lead transmuting into gold, the seven planets, the sealed vessel. It can look like a wall of ornament. It isn't. Strip the whole of it back and the alchemists were pointing at three things, which they called salt, sulphur, and mercury.

Body. Mind. Spirit.

That is all of it. Mercury is spirit — quicksilver, *argentum vivum*, living silver, so called because it moves on its own and cannot be held. Salt is body, the principle of what stays. And sulphur is the reconciling third between them — the mind, the moving life of thought and feeling. (The old texts call that middle term *soul*, but the word has been worn so thin, and pulled so many ways, that *mind* is the truer word for it now.) Dissolution is what separates the three: the spirit breaks loose from the body, and for a while you are not one integrated thing. Fixing is what marries them back together, and to fix, technically, is to fix the mercury — to take the living silver that dissolution set loose and bind it into something that stays.

You are not being asked to acquire anything. You are being asked to visit the interior of your own earth, let what is false come apart, and let the fire bind back into one substance the three you already were. Dissolve, then build — not once, as a single heroic crisis you survive and frame on the wall, but as a rhythm, over and over, a little deeper each turn. Every real practice breathes this way: in, and down, and apart; then up, and through, and whole.

The two Latin riddles were never rival maps. They're one breath.
