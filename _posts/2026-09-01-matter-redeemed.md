*The body isn't the obstacle to the knowing — it's the instrument of it.*

There is a moment, early in the contemplative life — right at the beginning, actually — when you decide the body is the problem.

It is an easy conclusion to reach. You sit to meditate and the knee aches, your back is sore, the mind will not settle, the world outside the window carries on being loud and unredeemed. The teaching seems to point up and away — beyond the senses, beyond the flesh — and so you draw the obvious inference: the body is ballast, the world a distraction, and whatever is holy must be whatever is left once you have finally risen above both.

I held some version of this for longer than I'd like to admit. It has a spiritual smell to it, which is exactly what makes it dangerous. It feels like renunciation. It is actually a kind of flinching.

One of the oldest streams of the tradition caught this error and named it. The Gnostics, in their bleaker moods, taught that matter was a prison and the spark in us a prisoner — that salvation meant escape, a getaway from creation. It is a seductive story, and it is a dead end. Because if matter is only a prison, then your hands, your breath, the ache in the knee, the person sitting across from you at breakfast — all of it is something to be survived rather than known. And a spirituality you have to survive your own life to practise is no spirituality at all.

The alchemists answered this, and their answer is the hinge of everything I want to say. They did not seek to flee matter. They sought its redemption — *redemptio* — the freeing of the light held captive inside the material world, not away from it. The stone they were after was not a ticket out. It was this world seen through to its depth. Spirit was not the alternative to matter; it was matter woken up.

This is not a metaphor I find charming. It is a description of what actually happens.

Here is the plain fact that thirty years of sitting has driven home: the deepest knowing does not arrive when I have escaped the body. It arrives through it. The subtle senses that open in real meditation are senses — perception, not abstraction. The awareness that finally steadies is an embodied awareness, rooted in the breath, grounded in the belly, alive in the very flesh I once wanted to rise above. Every time I have tried to reach the heights by abandoning the ground, I have always crashed back down to earth. The real thing has always come the other way: down into the body, until the body itself becomes transparent to something larger.

Matter is not the obstacle to the knowing. Matter is the site of it.

Once you see this, a great deal reorganises. The point of practice is no longer to accumulate exalted states and carry them off somewhere clean. The point is to let the light already sleeping in the ordinary — in the meal, the walk, the work, the grief, the washing-up — come awake. The stone is not found by leaving the world. It is found by staying, and looking, until the world gives up its secret. Maybe the truth of all this is carried in the statement: 'Before enlightenment, chop wood, carry water; after enlightenment, chop wood, carry water.'

I am about to test this claim rather more literally than usual. But that is for another letter.

Beyond belief — into knowing. It was never going to be found by leaving the ground.
